## 2. In short

Momento is an app for your own Nextcloud server. It only talks to the server you enter. The developer runs no server of their own, receives none of your photos, credentials or usage data, and cannot see them. There is no account with the developer, no advertising, no tracking and no analytics.

## 3. What data the app processes

### 3.1 Photos and videos

If you allow access to your photo library, Momento reads your photos and videos with their metadata (for example date taken, camera and location). It uses them to upload the photos to your Nextcloud server and to see what is already there. If you turn this on, Momento deletes photos from your device once they are uploaded. iOS asks you to confirm every time.

To find duplicate photos, the app compares images on your iPhone or iPad only (Apple Vision). No data leaves the device for this.

### 3.2 Data from your Nextcloud server

The app loads previews, originals, albums, shares, places, tags and, if set up there, people from face recognition from your server. This data is cached on your device so the app stays fast and works offline. You choose the cache size yourself.

Face recognition does not run in the app but on your server (the Nextcloud app “Recognize”). The operator of that server is responsible for it.

### 3.3 Login

You log in through the official Nextcloud login in the browser, so the app never sees your password. It gets an app password from the server and stores it, together with the server address and your user name, in the iOS Keychain. To remove this data, log out or delete the app. You can also revoke the app password at any time in your Nextcloud security settings.

### 3.4 Sharing to Momento

When you share photos or videos “to Momento” from another app, the share extension reads your login from the iOS keychain and uploads the files straight to your Nextcloud server. The files are kept on the device only briefly for this and deleted afterwards.

### 3.5 Momento Pro (subscription)

You buy Momento Pro as a subscription through the App Store. Apple handles payment and billing; the developer receives neither your name nor your payment details. The app only learns from Apple whether a subscription is active and remembers this locally on the device. Apple's privacy policy applies to purchases: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

### 3.6 Settings

Settings such as the cache size, recent searches or whether videos play muted are stored locally on your device only.

## 4. Where data is sent

- **Your Nextcloud server:** All photos, videos and metadata go only to the server address you enter, encrypted over HTTPS. The data there is the responsibility of the server's operator, which is you or, for example, your hosting provider. Their privacy policy applies.
- **Apple Maps:** The map view uses Apple MapKit. iOS loads map data from Apple, so Apple learns the map area shown and your IP address. The app does not send your photos or their location data to Apple. More at https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/
- **Apple App Store:** To buy, restore and check the subscription, iOS talks to the App Store (see 3.5).
- **Sharing:** When you share photos through the iOS share sheet, they go to the app or person you choose.
- **Crash reports:** If you allow iOS to share analytics with app developers, the developer receives anonymous crash reports through Apple. You control this under Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

There are no other recipients.

## 5. Legal basis

The app processes your data to provide the features you use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and on the basis of your consent, for example for access to your photo library (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw your consent at any time in the iOS settings.

## 6. Retention

All data on your device stays there until you log out, clear the cache or delete the app. How long data stays on your server is up to its operator.

## 7. Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection (Art. 15–21 GDPR). Because the developer holds no data about you, you exercise these rights for data in the app yourself by deleting the app. For data on your server, contact its operator.

You may also lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for example the one where you live.
